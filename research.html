Adaptive norm-based coding of within-person variability

Using an adaptation paradigm, we found that within-person variability for familiar faces are represented in reference to identity-specific averages. Adaptation to an anti-exemplar created through distorting identity-specific averages yielded larger adaptation effects than gender-matched averages. We show that familiar face representations have a norm-based architecture in the face space.

How do we organize facial identity in memory?

The problem of face recognition has two faces (pun very much intended): homogeneity between people and variability within person. The face space theory provides a solution to the problem of homogeneity but is limited in explaining the problem of variability. To understand how the face space represents variability, and by extension, face familiarity, we analyzed inter-item similarity ratings of naturalistic face images using multidimensional scaling. We show a 3-dimensional visual depiction of the face space that represents within-person variability. We found that familiarity compresses within-person variability in the face space, but does not increase the distance between identities.
