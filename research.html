From pictures to the people in them: Averaging within-person variability leads to face familiarization.

The human visual system is very good at extracting statistical summaries from the environment, a process called ensemble coding. We found that people use ensemble coding as they become familiar with a newly encountered face through exposure to different images of the same person by averaging their face over time.

  • Authors: Y. Koca, C. Oriet
  • Year: 2023

Crowding the face space: The attractor field hypothesis and within-person variability.

We found that when a face is learned among others that are highly similar to that face, our ability to tell that two pictures of that face depict the same person gets worse than if the face hasn't been seen at all. How do we get worse at remembering a face after we see them? We show that learning a face among others that are highly similar creates memory representations that are extremely conservative, making it difficult to accept two photos of that person as that person.

  • Authors: Y. Koca, R. L. Corpuz, C. Oriet
  • Year: 2024

Anchoring has little effect when forming first impressions of facial attractiveness.

Do first impressions matter? We showed people multiple images of the same person in either ascending, descending, or random order of attractiveness. Their attractiveness judgments were marginally impacted by presentation order.

  • Authors: R. S. S. Kramer, Y. Koca, M. O. Mireku, C. Oriet
  • Year: 2024

Is that BOB or is that the butcher? The role of context in the butcher-on-the-bus phenomenon.

You see someone, and you know you know them, but can't remember where. Butcher on the bus is a frustrating and common experience, but we don't know why it happens. We predicted that this experience stems from a strong association between a face and the context in which we frequently see them, with stronger associations for contexts that give information about a person (e.g., occupation). We found that faces learned under consistent infromative contexts led to better sensitivity recognizing them in new encounters, but were also more likely to elicit butcher on the bus.

  • Authors: L. Ueckert, Y. Koca, C. Oriet
  • Year: Under Review

Adaptive norm-based coding of within-person variability

Using an adaptation paradigm, we found that within-person variability for familiar faces are represented in reference to identity-specific averages. Adaptation to an anti-exemplar created through distorting identity-specific averages yielded larger adaptation effects than gender-matched averages. We show that familiar face representations have a norm-based architecture in the face space.

  • Authors: Y. Koca, C. Oriet
  • Year: In preparation

How do we organize facial identity in memory?

The problem of face recognition has two faces (pun very much intended): homogeneity between people and variability within person. The face space theory provides a solution to the problem of homogeneity but is limited in explaining the problem of variability. To understand how the face space represents variability, and by extension, face familiarity, we analyzed inter-item similarity ratings of naturalistic face images using multidimensional scaling. We show a 3-dimensional visual depiction of the face space that represents within-person variability. We found that familiarity compresses within-person variability in the face space, but does not increase the distance between identities.

  • Authors: Y. Koca, C. Oriet
  • Year: In preparation
